MYTHS
The Legends of Archimedes Go On
Archimedes was a brilliant Greek mathematician, scientist, and inventor who
lived in Syracuse, Sicily. King Hiero often asked him to solve difficult
problems. One day, the king wanted to know if his crown was made of pure
gold without melting it. While taking a bath, Archimedes noticed that water
overflowed when he stepped into the tub. This inspired him to discover a way
to test the crown, leading him to shout, "Eureka!" He proved that the crown
was not made of pure gold and discovered the principle of water
displacement, now known as the Archimedes Principle.
Archimedes also demonstrated how a small force could move a heavy ship by
using a system of pulleys. His discoveries led to the development of important
machines and scientific principles. He also improved the value of pi, invented
the Archimedean screw for lifting water, and created powerful war machines
like the catapult. His inventions and discoveries continue to influence science
and mathematics today.
Pandora’s Box
Long ago, the king of the gods, Zeus, wanted to punish humans for receiving fire from
Prometheus. He ordered the creation of Pandora, the first woman, and gave her a beautiful
jar. Zeus warned her never to open it.Pandora became curious about what was inside. One
day, she opened the jar, and terrible things such as sickness, sadness, fear, and anger
escaped into the world. Frightened, she quickly closed the jar, but only one thing remained
inside—Hope. Hope later came out to comfort people, reminding them that even during
difficult times, there is always hope.
Echo and Narcissus
Echo was a kind mountain nymph who loved to talk. Because she distracted the goddess
Hera, she was punished and could only repeat the last words spoken by others. One day,
Echo fell in love with a handsome young man named Narcissus. However, Narcissus
admired only himself and rejected everyone who loved him. As punishment for his pride, he
fell in love with his own reflection in a pool of water. Unable to leave it, he remained there
until he died, and a beautiful flower grew in his place. Echo’s voice continued to be heard
in the mountains.
King Midas and the Golden Touch
King Midas loved gold more than anything. One day, the god Dionysus granted him one
wish. Midas wished that everything he touched would turn into gold.At first, he was
delighted as trees, furniture, and stones became gold. But when he touched his food and
water, they also turned to gold, making it impossible to eat or drink. Even his beloved
daughter turned into a golden statue when he hugged her. Realizing his mistake, Midas
begged the god to remove the gift. Dionysus told him to wash in a river, and the golden
touch disappeared.
Daedalus and Icarus
Daedalus was a skilled inventor who built wings from feathers and wax for himself and his
son, Icarus, so they could escape from an island. Before they flew, Daedalus warned Icarus
not to fly too close to the sun or too close to the sea.
Excited by flying, Icarus ignored his father’s warning and soared higher and higher. The
sun melted the wax holding his wings together, and he fell into the sea. Daedalus safely
reached land but sadly lost his son because he did not listen.
Demeter and Persephone
Persephone, the daughter of the harvest goddess Demeter, was taken to the underworld by
Hades. Demeter became so sad that plants stopped growing, and the world became cold
and barren.Zeus ordered Hades to let Persephone return to her mother. However, because
Persephone had eaten a few pomegranate seeds in the underworld, she had to spend part
of each year with Hades. Whenever Persephone returned to Demeter, flowers bloomed and
crops grew. When she went back to the underworld, the plants withered.
The Blessings of the Banyan Tree
A Myth Adapted by the Author
In a small village, there stood an ancient banyan tree believed to be a source of
blessings. The villagers often visited the tree to give thanks for their good fortune, knowing
that the tree had the power to bring prosperity to those who expressed their gratitude
sincerely. The treee was a symbol of hope and abundance, its roots deep and branches wide,
providing shade and shelter to all who sought it.
One year, a terrible drought struck the village. The once fertile fields turned barren, and
the river that flowed through the village dried up.
Food and water became scarce, and the villagers grew despeerate. Despite their
hardships, they gathered around the banyan tree offering prayers and gratitude for any small
blessings they still had.
Among them was a young girl named Mira, who always expressed her gratitude
eloquently. Mira's family had taught her the importance of being thankful, even in difficult
times.
She approached the tree and said, "Thank you, dear banyan tree for the strength to
endure and the hope to see another day. We are grateful for the little we have and for the
promise of better times."
Moved by the villagers' gratitude, the banyan tree's roots reached deep into the earth,
searching for water. After days of effort, the roots found a hidden underground spring. Water
began to flow, revitalizing the village.
Years passed, and the banyan tree grew even more majestic, its branches spreading
wide to provide shelter and comfort. The villagers continued to gather beneath it's shade,
sharing stories of gratitude and blessings. The banyan tree became a symbol of their
enduring faith and the power of thankfulness.
Mira, now an elder in the village, reflected on the lessons she had learned. She realized
that gratitude was not just about the words they spoke but also about the actions they took
to received and created a legacy of kindness and generosity.
The Trojan Horse
Long ago, the Greeks and the Trojans fought a war for ten years. The Greeks wanted to
rescue Helen, the queen of Sparta, who had been taken to the city of Troy. However, Troy
was protected by strong, high walls that the Greek army could not break through.
One day, the clever Greek general Odysseus came up with a plan. He told the Greeks to
build a giant wooden horse that was hollow inside. A group of brave Greek soldiers hid
inside the horse while the rest of the army pretended to sail away, making it seem as if they
had given up the war.
When the Trojans saw that the Greeks were gone, they believed they had won. They found
the large wooden horse outside the city gates and thought it was a peace offering. They
happily pulled the horse into the city and celebrated their victory.
That night, after everyone in Troy had fallen asleep, the Greek soldiers climbed out of the
wooden horse. They quietly opened the city gates, allowing the rest of the Greek army, who
had secretly returned, to enter the city. The Greeks surprised the Trojans and captured Troy,
ending the long war.
Sif’s Golden Hair
Loki, the mischievous brother of Odin, loved causing trouble wherever he went. One day,
he saw Sif, the wife of Thor, sleeping peacefully. Sif was famous for her long, beautiful
golden hair, which she treasured. While she slept, Loki secretly cut off all her hair just for
fun.
When Sif woke up and discovered what had happened, she cried because she loved her
golden hair and feared that Thor would be angry. When Thor found out that Loki was
responsible, he became furious. He confronted Loki and threatened to punish him unless
he found a way to replace Sif’s hair. Frightened, Loki promised to make things right.
To keep his promise, Loki traveled to the underground world of the skilled elves. He asked
them to create new hair for Sif using pure gold. The elves carefully crafted golden strands
that were soft, beautiful, and looked just like real hair.
Loki returned to Asgard and placed the golden hair on Sif’s head. To everyone’s
amazement, the golden hair magically grew and became part of her, looking even more
beautiful than before. Sif was happy again, and Thor forgave Loki for his mischief.
Odysseus and the Cyclops
After the Trojan War, Odysseus and his men sailed home. During their journey, they landed
on an island that was home to the Cyclopes, a race of giant one-eyed creatures. They
entered a large cave, ate some food they found there, and waited, not knowing it belonged
to a Cyclops named Polyphemus.
When Polyphemus returned, he trapped Odysseus and his men inside the cave by rolling a
huge stone over the entrance. The giant was cruel and began eating two of Odysseus’
men every day. Since only the Cyclops was strong enough to move the stone, Odysseus
knew he had to think of a clever way to escape.
Odysseus came up with a plan. He offered Polyphemus strong wine, which made the giant
fall into a deep sleep. Before the Cyclops fell asleep, he asked Odysseus for his name.
Odysseus cleverly replied, “My name is No Man.”
While Polyphemus slept, Odysseus and his remaining men sharpened a large wooden
stake,
heated it in the fire, and drove it into the Cyclops’ only eye. The giant cried out in pain and
called for help. When the other Cyclopes asked who had hurt him, he answered, “No Man!
” Thinking no one had attacked him, they left without helping.
The next morning, the blind Cyclops let his sheep out to graze. To escape, Odysseus tied
each of his men underneath the sheep, while he held onto the last sheep himself.
Polyphemus only felt the sheep’s backs, so he did not notice the men hiding underneath.
In this way, Odysseus and his crew escaped safely.
As they sailed away, Odysseus proudly shouted his real name to Polyphemus, revealing
who had tricked him. The story shows that intelligence, courage, and quick thinking can
help overcome great danger, but it also reminds us that pride can sometimes lead to
trouble.
Narcissus
Long ago in Ancient Greece, a river god named Cephissus and his wife Liriope had a son
named Narcissus. He grew up to be a very handsome and strong young man. Before he was
born, a wise soothsayer named Teiresias predicted that Narcissus would live a long life only
if he never saw his own reflection. To protect him, his mother hid all the mirrors and
covered the windows in their home.
As Narcissus grew older, he met a kind nymph named Echo, who fell deeply in love with
him. However, Echo had been cursed by the goddess Hera. Because of the curse, she could
only repeat the last words spoken by others and could not express her true feelings. When
Narcissus asked if she liked him, Echo could only repeat his last words. Narcissus
misunderstood her and thought she only cared about herself, so he turned away.
Heartbroken, Echo left in sadness.
Later, Narcissus went to a quiet river to be alone. As he looked into the still water, he saw
his own reflection for the first time. Not realizing it was himself, he believed he was looking
at another beautiful person. He became so fascinated by the image that he could not look
away.
The prophecy of Teiresias came true. Narcissus remained by the river, staring at his
reflection for so long that the gods transformed him into a beautiful flower. That flower
became known as the narcissus, which still grows in the same place according to the myth.
The Sky Woman
Long ago, the world was covered with water, and there was no sun, moon, or stars. High
above the earth lived the Great Spirit and his daughter, Sky Woman. One day, the Great
Spirit sent Sky Woman down to the world below. As she fell, the water animals worked
together to help her. After many attempts, the muskrat brought up a small amount of earth
from beneath the water. The animals placed it on the back of a giant turtle, where it grew
into a large land for Sky Woman to live on.
Sky Woman later gave birth to twin sons known as the Good Spirit and the Evil Spirit. After
her death, the Good Spirit created the sun, moon, stars, plants, animals, rivers, and the
first human. The Evil Spirit tried to ruin his brother’s creations by making dangerous
animals, poisonous plants, and other harmful things.
The two brothers eventually fought, and the Good Spirit defeated the Evil Spirit and
banished him underground. Even so, the Evil Spirit’s influence remained in the world.
