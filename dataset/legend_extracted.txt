LEGENDS
The Legend of Maria Makiling
Maria Makiling was a beautiful and kind fairy who lived on the slopes of Mount Makiling.
She helped the poor by giving them food, fruits, and medicine whenever they needed
help. She also protected the forest and its animals. However, some people became
greedy and abused the gifts of the mountain by cutting trees and taking more than they
needed. Saddened by their actions, Maria Makiling disappeared. It is said that she still
watches over the mountain, rewarding those who respect nature and hiding from those
who destroy it.
The Legend of King Arthur
Long ago in Britain, there was no true king to rule the land. One day, a mysterious sword
appeared, firmly fixed in a stone. Written on the stone were the words that whoever could
pull out the sword was the rightful king of England.
Many powerful knights tried to remove the sword but failed. A young boy named Arthur, who
had been raised by Sir Ector, pulled the sword from the stone with ease while trying to help
his foster brother. The people were amazed and accepted Arthur as their king.
As king, Arthur ruled wisely and gathered the bravest knights around the Round Table.
Together they protected the kingdom, fought for justice, and helped those in need. Arthur
became known as one of Britain’s greatest kings.
Robin Hood
Robin Hood lived in Sherwood Forest with his loyal friends. He was a skilled archer who
believed the poor should be treated fairly.
The Sheriff of Nottingham collected heavy taxes from the poor while keeping the riches for
himself. Robin Hood and his companions stopped the sheriff’s men, took back the
unfairly collected money, and shared it with those who needed it most.
Robin Hood became famous throughout England as a hero who fought against injustice
and defended the poor.
The Legend of Sleepy Hollow
In the quiet village of Sleepy Hollow lived a schoolteacher named Ichabod Crane. The
villagers often spoke of the mysterious Headless Horseman, who was said to ride through
the village at night.
One evening, after attending a party, Ichabod rode home alone. Suddenly, he saw a dark
rider carrying what appeared to be a head. Frightened, Ichabod raced away, but the
horseman chased him until a pumpkin was thrown in his direction.
The next morning, Ichabod had disappeared. Some believed he had escaped, while others
believed the Headless Horseman had taken him away.
The Legend of the Pineapple
A young girl named Pina was very lazy and depended on her mother for everything. One day,
while her mother was sick, Pina refused to help prepare the meal. Her mother became
upset and wished that Pina had many eyes so she could find things on her own.
Soon after, Pina disappeared. In the place where she had been standing grew a strange fruit
covered with many small “eyes.” The people called it a pineapple, remembering Pina
and the lesson to become responsible and helpful.
The Legend of Mount Mayon
Daragang Magayon was a beautiful young woman admired by many. She fell in love with
Panganoron, a brave warrior. However, another chief wanted Magayon for himself and
caused conflict between the two men.
During the battle, Panganoron was struck by an arrow while protecting Magayon.
Heartbroken, Magayon embraced him, and they both died. The people buried them
together. Later, a magnificent volcano rose where they had been buried. The people
believed it was Mount Mayon, whose perfect shape symbolized Magayon’s beauty and
the couple’s everlasting love.
The Tale of the Reverent Son
A Legend Adapted by the Author
Long ago, in a peaceful village nestled between mountains, there lived a body named
Akiro. He was known throughout the village for his respect and politeness, especially
towards his elders. His parents had taught him the importance of using proper language
when speaking to others, instilling in him the values of honor and reverence.
One day, an old sage visited the village. The sage was wise and had traveled far and wide,
sharing his knowledge with those he met. The villagers gathered to listen to his stories, eager
to learn from his experiences. As the sage spoke l, the villagers were captivated by his tales
of distant lands and ancient wisdom.
However, it was Akiro who caught the sage's attention. The young boy approached the
sage with a deep bow, his eyes filled with respect. "Honored sage," he began, "it is a privilege
to hear your wisdom. May I ask you to share a story with us about the importance of respect?"
The sage smiled and nodded. He began to tell the villagers a story about a great kingdom
where respect and polite language were the foundation of a harmonious society.
The sage's story went on to describe how the kingdom thrived because of the mutual
respect shared among its people. Whenever disagreement arose, they were settled calmly
through understanding and kindness, allowing the people to stay close and work together.
The sage then looked at Akiro and said, "Young boy, your respect and courteous language
remind me of the people in that kingdom. It is a rare and precious quality that you possess."
From that day on, Akiro became an example for all the children in the village. They began
to emulate his respectful behaviour, using polite language and showing reverence to their
elders. The village thrived, and the bonds between families grew stronger. The villagers found
that respectful communication led to greater understanding and cooperation.
Akiro's paretns were proud of their son, and they taught him that respect was not just
about words, but also about actions. Akiro continued to oive by these principles, earning the
respect and admiration of everyone around him. He helped his neighbours with their chores,
listened attentively when others spoke, and always greeted people with a smile.
As Akiro grew older, he became a respected leader in the village. He taught the younger
generation the importance of respect and polite language, ensuring that the values of honor
and reverence were passed down. The village continued to prospee, its people united by the
bonds of respect and mutual understanding.
One evening, as the sun set behind the mountains, Akiro reflected on the sage's visit the
impact it had on his life. He realized that respect was a cornerstone of a strong community
and that by honoring others with kind words and actions, they could build a better world
together.
The Legend of the Sun and the Moon
In the beginning, the Sun and the Moon lived together in the sky. The Sun was bright and
warm, while the Moon was gentle and cool. They loved being close, but the Sun’s light was
too strong, and the world became too hot.
The Moon decided to move farther away, so that day and night could take turns. The Sun
was sad but understood that this balance was needed for life to thrive.That is why today, the
Sun shines during the day, and the Moon glows at night.
The Legend of Malakas and Maganda
Long ago, a giant bamboo stalk floated on the sea. When it split open, out came Malakas
(Strong) and Maganda (Beautiful). They were the first man and woman. Together, they
learned to live on land, planting crops, fishing, and building a home. Soon, they had children,
and their children had more children. From them came the Filipino people.
The Legend of Mayon Volcano
Long ago in Albay, a beautiful maiden named Daragang Magayon was loved by many.
She fell in love with a brave warrior named Panganoron. But another man, Patuga, wanted
her for himself. Patuga kidnapped Magayon’s father to force her into marriage.
Panganoron fought to save him, but in the battle, both Magayon and Panganoron were
struck down. The people buried them together, and from their grave rose a perfect cone-
shaped volcano Mayon Volcano, symbolizing their eternal love.
The Legend of the Bakunawa
In ancient times, the sky had seven moons. People admired their beauty, but a giant sea
serpent called Bakunawa grew jealous. One night, it leapt from the ocean and swallowed a
moon. Night after night, Bakunawa ate more moons until only one remained. The people
prayed and banged pots and pans to scare the serpent away. Bakunawa fled, leaving the last
moon to shine.
The Legend of the Mango Tree
A poor farmer once prayed for food to feed his family. The gods answered by planting a tree
in his yard. It bore golden fruit sweet and nourishing. The farmer shared the mangoes with
his neighbors, and soon the whole village thrived. But when greedy men tried to hoard the
fruit, the tree stopped bearing. Only when the people learned to share again did the mango
tree bloom once more.
